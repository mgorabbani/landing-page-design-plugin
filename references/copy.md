# Copy — headlines, sub-headlines, CTAs, anti-patterns

AI-slop isn't just visual. Half of it is the words. "Build something amazing." "Stop wasting time, start winning." "The all-in-one platform for modern teams." These lines are cheap to write and they drain every site they appear on. This reference gives you patterns that work and a list of phrases to never use.

## The one rule that matters

**Every line earns its place by being specific.** Generic boosterism is slop. Specificity is design.

Bad: *"The fastest way to ship."*
Better: *"Deploy in 90 seconds. Keep your existing repo."*

Bad: *"Beautiful, powerful, simple."*
Better: *"Invoicing for freelancers. One page, no logins."*

If a line could appear on any SaaS landing page in the world, rewrite it.

## Hero headlines by domain

For each domain: the shape that works, plus 2 concrete examples (worked, didn't work).

### Medical / health practice

**Shape:** Practitioner-focused or outcome-focused. Never clever.
- Works: *"Dermatology in Dubai Marina — by appointment."* / *"Dr. Sana Rahim · Cosmetic and laser dermatology"*
- Doesn't: *"Skin, reimagined."* (clever, undermines trust)

### SaaS / dev tool

**Shape:** One-sentence clarity of what + for whom. Sub-head adds the differentiator.
- Works: *"The monitoring tool for side projects."* / sub: *"One-line install. Free below 1M events."*
- Doesn't: *"Observability, reimagined for the modern stack."* (generic)

### Agency / studio

**Shape:** Short, opinionated, often a statement of identity rather than a sales pitch.
- Works: *"We design brands for companies that make things."* / *"Studio Marchetti — brand and type, Milan + Lisbon"*
- Doesn't: *"Award-winning digital experiences that drive results."* (could be anyone)

### E-commerce

**Shape:** Product-name-first or category-first. Price / scarcity when relevant.
- Works: *"The everyday chore coat. Waxed cotton, made in Portugal."* / *"Ships this week · £285"*
- Doesn't: *"Elevate your wardrobe."* (nothing said)

### Portfolio

**Shape:** Name + role + specialty. Subhead is where + availability.
- Works: *"Leila Tazi — Product designer specialising in payments."* / *"Lisbon · Available June 2026"*
- Doesn't: *"Hi, I'm a creative passionate about building great experiences."* (cringe)

### Restaurant

**Shape:** Name + cuisine + one atmospheric line. Not hard-selling.
- Works: *"Osteria del Porto — Ligurian cooking, 24 seats, walk the hill."*
- Doesn't: *"An unforgettable culinary journey."* (every restaurant ever)

### Creator / course

**Shape:** Outcome-first. What will the student be able to do, specifically.
- Works: *"Learn to typeset a book by hand — 6 weeks, one cohort a year."* / sub: *"For editors and designers moving into book work."*
- Doesn't: *"Master the art of design."* (meaningless)

### Nonprofit

**Shape:** Mission statement, not slogan.
- Works: *"We build schools in rural Kenya. 42 so far."* / sub: *"Every donation funds a named room."*
- Doesn't: *"Together, we can make a difference."* (applies to every nonprofit)

### Local service

**Shape:** Service + city + one trust signal. Phone number close.
- Works: *"Licensed electricians in Austin since 2004."* / *"24/7 emergency · 512-555-0142"*
- Doesn't: *"Your trusted partner for all electrical needs."* (could be anyone)

### Crypto / DeFi

**Shape:** Technical and specific. Chain + category + metric, not hype.
- Works: *"Lending protocol on Base. $142M TVL · Audited by Trail of Bits."*
- Doesn't: *"Redefine DeFi for the next generation."* (AI-slop)

### AI product

**Shape:** What it does, concretely. Skip "AI-powered" — assume.
- Works: *"Translate technical docs between English, Japanese, and German — preserving code."*
- Doesn't: *"AI-powered productivity, reimagined."* (three AI-slop words in one line)

### Real estate

**Shape:** Agent + market + one signal of standing.
- Works: *"Mara Chen — residential broker, Brooklyn brownstones. 38 closings in 2025."*
- Doesn't: *"Your trusted real estate partner."* (every agent website)

## Sub-headline patterns

The sub-head adds the one differentiator that makes the headline land. Keep it to one sentence, under 15 words.

- Pattern: *"[Audience] use [product] to [specific outcome]."* — *"Freelance designers use Scribe to send invoices from their phone."*
- Pattern: *"[Quantified proof] + [timeframe]."* — *"90-second deploy. Free under 1M events."*
- Pattern: *"[Specific constraint] + [payoff]."* — *"One screen. No logins. Works offline."*
- Pattern: *"[Uncommon qualifier] + [common category]."* — *"The only note-taking app designed for writers who read aloud."*

Anti-pattern: restating the headline. If your headline is "The fastest way to ship," don't sub with "Ship fast with our blazing-fast platform."

## CTA button labels

The CTA is a promise. Be specific about what happens when they click.

### Works (specific)
- *Book consultation* — doctor
- *Start free — no card* — SaaS
- *See the curriculum* — creator
- *Request a quote* — local service
- *Connect wallet* — crypto
- *Schedule a viewing* — real estate
- *View the menu* — restaurant
- *Download CV* — portfolio
- *Donate £5 today* — nonprofit
- *Add to cart — £285* — e-com

### Doesn't (generic)
- *Get started*
- *Learn more*
- *Click here*
- *Submit*
- *Sign up*

"Get started" is the most-used CTA on the web and the least specific. If you catch yourself writing it, replace with a verb + object that describes the actual first action.

## Feature-section copy

Each feature needs: **title (≤5 words) · description (1 sentence, ≤20 words)**.

- Good title: *"Offline by default"* · good desc: *"Everything works in flight mode. Changes sync when you reconnect."*
- Bad title: *"Powerful sync capabilities"* · bad desc: *"Our powerful sync engine keeps all your data up-to-date everywhere with best-in-class reliability."*

Write the description as if to a skeptical reader who's seen a hundred landing pages this week. Specific verbs. Concrete outcomes.

## Testimonial selection

If the user gives you quotes, pick the ones that are **specific and against type**:

- Good: *"I'd been putting off invoicing for three weeks. Used Scribe on a plane. Sent 11 invoices in 20 minutes. Got paid before I landed."* — Sarah Chen, freelance illustrator
- Bad: *"Scribe has transformed how I manage my business. Couldn't live without it!"* — Sarah Chen

The first one makes the reader see a specific scene. The second is noise. If you only have noise-tier testimonials, ask the user for real stories before rendering the section.

## Anti-patterns — phrases to never ship

Any line containing one of these is a rewrite candidate:

- *"reimagined"*
- *"next-generation"*
- *"best-in-class"*
- *"powerful yet simple"*
- *"beautiful and intuitive"*
- *"the all-in-one platform"*
- *"take your [X] to the next level"*
- *"game-changing"*
- *"cutting-edge"*
- *"seamlessly"*
- *"empowering [audience] to [verb]"*
- *"built from the ground up"*
- *"industry-leading"*
- *"robust and scalable"*
- *"world-class"*
- *"your trusted partner"*
- *"we're passionate about..."*
- *"unlock the power of..."*
- *"AI-powered"* as the main selling point (show what it does instead)
- Any line ending with *", period."*
- Any line structured as *"[Adjective]. [Adjective]. [Adjective]."* (the three-word tagline tell)

## Writing order

1. Write the headline. Rewrite it 3 times until it's specific.
2. Write the sub-head. It says the one thing the headline leaves out.
3. Write the CTA. Verb + concrete object.
4. Write the feature titles. 5 words max.
5. Write the feature descriptions. One sentence.
6. Write everything else.

If you can't write the headline specifically, you don't yet understand what the site is for. Stop and ask the user one sharpening question before you continue.

## The handoff check

Before declaring copy done, re-read every headline and sub-head as a skeptic. Ask for each:

- Could this appear on any competitor's site unchanged? (If yes, rewrite.)
- Does it name a specific person, product, place, number, or outcome? (If none, rewrite.)
- Is it shorter than the first draft? (If not, cut.)
